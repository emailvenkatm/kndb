Healthcare — five real cases

Ambient AI

Nabla / Whisper hallucinations written into signed clinical notes

~85 US health systems · ~7M encounters · Fortune, Oct 26 2024

Whisper-based ambient scribes hallucinated content into notes signed by clinicians. Cornell and University of Washington researchers logged hallucinations in ~1% of ~13,000 clean audio segments. A Michigan study hit them in 8 of 10 samples. Nabla's CTO confirmed the source audio is deliberately deleted "for data safety." Once a fabricated finding is signed, no ground truth remains to audit against.

Why KNDB: canonical AI-generated-as-observed collision at the storage boundary. The distinction between "the doctor said it" and "the model wrote it" is erased at write time. KNDB's epistemic-kind primitive is the missing enforcement.
Source: fortune.com/2024/10/26/openai-transcription-tool-whisper-hallucination-rate-ai-tools-hospitals-patients-doctors
Litigation

Sharp / Sutter / MemorialCare ambient-scribe class actions

Sharp: Nov 2025 · Washington v. Sutter Health, N.D. Cal. 4:26-cv-03012, Apr 2026

San Diego class action against Sharp HealthCare alleges Abridge recorded encounters without consent, and that EHR notes contained boilerplate stating the patient had consented when no such conversation occurred. A parallel federal suit against Sutter and MemorialCare (Northern District of California) targets the same pattern. The alleged fabricated consent language was model-generated boilerplate stored without any tag distinguishing it from clinician-authored text.

Why KNDB: discovery-grade proof that provenance-collapsed storage carries legal exposure. If the note carried an epistemic-kind tag, the fabricated consent would be visible to reviewers, plaintiffs, and defendants alike.
Source: hipaajournal.com — lawsuit AI platform illegally recorded patient-clinician conversations
Peer-reviewed

Epic Sepsis Model external validation failure

38,455 Michigan Medicine hospitalizations · JAMA Intern Med 2021;181(8):1065

Wong et al. externally validated the vendor-shipped Epic Sepsis Model at Michigan Medicine. It missed ~two-thirds of sepsis cases and drove massive alert fatigue. The ESM score is written to the EHR every fifteen minutes. Downstream analytics consumed those scores as if they were observed risk signals, and the model was inherited by hundreds of hospitals before independent validation existed.

Why KNDB: numeric model output stored alongside vitals with no visible epistemic_kind = inference marker. Every downstream cohort study that used ESM as a feature quietly conflated model outputs with observations.
Source: jamanetwork.com — Wong et al., JAMA Intern Med 2021
Regulator

FDA Predetermined Change Control Plan final guidance

Finalized 14 February 2025

FDA finalized its guidance on "Predetermined Change Control Plans for Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Device Software Functions." Scope broadened from ML-only to all AI-DSF, but the final guidance does not require distinguishing AI-generated from measured data downstream of the device. It regulates the model, not what the model writes.

Why KNDB: the top US AI/ML device regulator explicitly stopped short of a storage-layer provenance mandate. That is the gap KNDB fills.
Source: fda.gov — PCCP final guidance
Regulator

ONC HTI-1 rule, Predictive DSI source attributes

89 FR 1192, published 9 Jan 2024 · DSI criterion effective 1 Jan 2025

The HTI-1 rule mandates 31 source attributes for Predictive DSIs and 13 for evidence-based DSIs. These attributes describe the model (training data, intended use, output type) — they do not require that a value produced by a Predictive DSI carry a persistent algorithm-origin flag when written to the chart.

Why KNDB: HTI-1 is often cited as "the AI-transparency answer." Model-level transparency plus provenance-less storage still produces observation/inference collision at query time. KNDB is the missing storage-layer half.
Source: mintz.com — HTI-1 final rule analysis

Cross-industry — five real cases

Litigation

DZ Reserve v. Meta — "Potential Reach" modeled but sold as measured

N.D. Cal. 3:18-cv-04978 · active 2025-2026~$7B damages sought

Advertiser plaintiffs allege that Meta's "Potential Reach" audience-size metric was inflated 200-400% because it counted duplicate and fake accounts. It was surfaced in the ad-buying API as if it were a measured audience count at bid time. Court records show internal executive acknowledgment of the inflation. Ninth Circuit affirmed class certification. Supreme Court declined to intervene. Jury trial vacated October 2025; next-steps hearing December 4, 2025.

Why KNDB: a derived estimate traveled through the API indistinguishable from a measured count. No epistemic-kind tag forced callers to treat it as inference. Textbook motivating case.
Source: cohenmilstein.com — DZ Reserve et al. v. Facebook · searchengineland.com
Business collapse

Zillow Offers — Zestimate consumed as purchase-price signal

iBuying shutdown Nov 2021~$569M writedown, 25% layoffs

Zillow shut down its iBuying arm in November 2021 after inventory write-downs and a 25% workforce cut. Root cause: the algorithmic Zestimate was treated as sufficient signal to bid at market. Downstream systems never distinguished "model prediction" from "verified comparable." Stanford GSB frames it as a lemons problem: algorithmic estimates used where deeper appraisal was required.

Why KNDB: model score consumed downstream as market price. A single epistemic_kind = inference tag on the Zestimate row would have forced the bidding system to run a policy check before trusting it as a price.
Source: gsb.stanford.edu — flip-flop Zillow post-mortem
Enforcement

CFPB v. Hello Digit — ML estimate executed as authorized ACH debit

August 2022$2.7M penalty + $68,145 redress · ~70,000 overdraft claims

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau fined Hello Digit $2.7 million plus $68,145 in redress. The autosave algorithm estimated a "safe" transfer amount for each customer. Downstream banking rails executed those estimates as authorized ACH debits. Roughly 70,000 overdraft reimbursement requests were filed since 2017.

Why KNDB: a machine-learning prediction of affordability was materialized into an authorized, measured banking transaction. Provenance did not survive the API boundary between the algorithm and the payments rail.
Source: consumerfinance.gov — CFPB v. Hello Digit press release
Live systemic

GA4 Blended reporting — modeled events silently mixed with observed events

Ongoing 2024-2026 · production analytics stack

Google's default GA4 "Blended" reporting identity injects modeled conversions into the same report rows as observed events once thresholds activate. Multiple 2024-2026 analyses document 15-70% attribution gaps across ad platforms. Meta reports ~26% higher conversions than analytics tools; Google Ads over-attributes 15-20% under Enhanced Conversions and Consent Mode V2. Ad teams cannot tell which conversions per row were measured versus modeled.

Why KNDB: the current, live version of the problem. Production analytics store that structurally cannot express kind. Every team's attribution numbers diverge because every team's "de-blending" heuristic is different.
Source: easyinsights.ai attribution-gaps analysis · plausible.io on GA4 modeled data
Peer-reviewed

Sambasivan et al., "Data Cascades" — peer-reviewed corroboration

CHI 2021 · 53 AI practitioners across three continents · DOI 10.1145/3411764.3445518

A CHI 2021 field study of 53 AI practitioners documents how early data-provenance failures compound downstream. The paper grounds the premise that data-quality issues — including imputation and silent defaults — are structurally under-tracked and cause outsized production incidents.

Why KNDB: peer-reviewed evidence that "impute-then-store without provenance" is a systemic MLOps failure mode, not one bad team on one bad day.
Source: DOI 10.1145/3411764.3445518 (ACM DOI page returned 403 at fetch time; verified via search snippet and Shankar et al. 2024 follow-up at arxiv.org/pdf/2209.09125). Re-verify via institutional network before submission.
03

The regulators see the gap. None of them have closed it.

The gap KNDB fills

FDA's PCCP guidance (Feb 2025) and ONC's HTI-1 DSI rule (effective Jan 2025) both regulate the model — training data, intended use, change management. Neither requires that a value written back to the chart carry a persistent epistemic-kind flag. The storage-layer provenance gap is where reality still bites, and it is exactly what KNDB targets. Adjacent efforts (HL7 FHIR Provenance resource, W3C PROV, DCAT-AP) sit at the record or dataset level, not the per-value level, and adoption is inconsistent.

Do not cite these — misclassification traps

Cases the paper might be tempted to cite as accuracy examples, but which are actually consent or right-of-publicity cases. Citing them for accuracy invites a rebuttal.

Trap 1 · ZoomInfo

Ramos et al. · ZoomInfo $29.55M settlement (Nov 2024). This is a right-of-publicity settlement, not a data-accuracy case. The complaint is that ZoomInfo used peoples' names and profiles in marketing preview UIs without permission. It says nothing about whether the underlying data was measured or inferred.

Trap 2 · Apollo.io

Apollo.io Illinois class action. Consent-based (BIPA-style), not accuracy. Same pattern: users' data was scraped and displayed, but the case is about whether they gave permission, not about whether the scraped values were correct.
